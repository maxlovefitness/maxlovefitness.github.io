how it works

Four moves. One cycle. Every week.

This is the entire system. Most people already know it. Almost nobody does all four at once, for long enough.

  1. Count everything.

    Every meal, every day. It was the main factor in my fat loss, more than any lift. The food-log prompt does the counting for you.

  2. Two lifts carry your upper body.

    A weighted pull-up opens one day, a weighted dip opens the other. Everything after them is support, and support can be sloppy.

  3. Two hard sets. Three to five minutes rest.

    Sixteen comfortable sets lose to two you actually mean. If you're not resting long enough to get bored, you're not lifting heavy enough to need it.

  4. Write it down.

    Lift, weight, both sets. Both hit eight, add five pounds. That page is the program, and it's the only thing separating people who change from people who attend.

progressive overload

Your bar isn't fixed.

Repeating a weight isn't standing still. A hard five becoming a clean eight on the same load is the progress; the plate change is just the receipt. Tell it where you are.

a note on who this is for

This isn't for everyone.

If you want to get huge, this isn't it. I'm not huge. If you want a six-day split and a reason to be at the gym for ninety minutes, this will bore you too. It's counting every meal, two lifts, two sets, and the same page filled in for a long time.

If you want to be lean and strong with a job, a life, and about forty minutes two or three times a week, this is the most you can get from the least. It worked on me. It works on you if you count and you show up.

questions

The ones everyone asks first.

Is two hard sets really enough?

For the first year or two, comfortably. You don't have a volume problem, you have an intensity problem — sixteen comfortable sets lose to two you actually mean, fully rested. Add volume later, once the weight has genuinely stopped moving.

Can I do this at home or a park?

Yes. A bar, something to dip on, and a backpack with books until you buy a belt. That's the whole gym. Every accessory can be dumbbells, bands, or skipped in month one.

How long until I see something?

Strength moves first, the mirror second, other people third. Judge the session count, not the reflection: twenty-four sessions with the belt heavier than when you started means it's working.

Do you use an app?

No. I paste my sets into ChatGPT and it keeps the log, compares to last time and tells me what to lift next. The two prompts that do it are free on the start-here page.
